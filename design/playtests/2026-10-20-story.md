## What worked

The physical clues establish uncertainty without requiring an explanation: dry hair, undisturbed gravel, the dog, incompatible photographs, and the missing recorded voice. Wren's letter resolves the identity question clearly. Her reply then gives the living woman her own boundaries instead of treating her as a plot device.

The memorial confrontation made defending Sam emotionally meaningful. The ledger later recalled that decision and both refused memory transfers accurately. The notebook retained the letter and Wren's reply, making the central reveal easy to review.

The later bargains are understandable before commitment. Dianne's tune explicitly changes ownership; Priya's memory remains hers if refused; the register preview explains harm to Wren's local history and Sam before offering a way back. Preparing a seat and a correspondence address makes departure tangible. Sam saying “Rowan” and the mileage log recording that name give The Stranger an earned resolution. Barb's explicit alternative of staying as oneself prevents departure from feeling like mandatory self-erasure.
